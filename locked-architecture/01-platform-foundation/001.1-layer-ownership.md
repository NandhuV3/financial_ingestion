# Layer Ownership

Status: LOCKED

---

# 1. Purpose

This document is the architectural constitution of the Ownership Intelligence Platform.

It defines:

- why every layer exists
- what every layer owns
- what every layer must never own
- how intelligence flows through the platform
- how evidence becomes ownership understanding

This document intentionally avoids implementation details.

Implementation belongs in the individual layer specifications.

This document defines ownership.

Whenever ownership conflicts arise, this document takes precedence.

---

# 2. Platform Mission

The platform does **not** exist to summarize SEC filings.

The platform does **not** exist to generate investment advice.

The platform exists to transform raw regulatory disclosures into structured, evidence-backed ownership intelligence.

The platform answers the five Ownership Questions through a sequence of independent architectural layers.

Each layer owns exactly one responsibility.

No layer performs work belonging to another layer.

---

# 3. Design Philosophy

The platform follows five fundamental principles.

## Principle 1

Evidence always flows forward.

Evidence never flows backward.

Every downstream conclusion must be traceable to upstream evidence.

---

## Principle 2

Every architectural responsibility has exactly one owner.

If two layers perform the same responsibility, the architecture is incorrect.

---

## Principle 3

Layers become increasingly abstract.

Raw Filing

↓

Evidence

↓

Observations

↓

Business Understanding

↓

Business Interpretation

↓

Ownership Understanding

---

## Principle 4

LLM reasoning is isolated.

Only designated reasoning layers may perform semantic reasoning.

All remaining layers remain deterministic.

---

## Principle 5

Governance owns durability.

Reasoning layers may propose.

Governance decides.

Nothing becomes durable without governance.

---

# 4. The Four Intelligence Stages

The platform transforms information through four distinct reasoning stages.

```
Observation

↓

Business Understanding

↓

Business Interpretation

↓

Ownership Understanding
```

Each stage has exactly one owner.

| Stage | Owner |
|---------|-------|
| Observation | Themes |
| Business Understanding | Structured Intelligence |
| Business Interpretation | Quarter Understanding |
| Ownership Understanding | Investor Intelligence |

No other layer may perform work belonging to one of these stages.

# 6. Architectural Categories

The platform is divided into six architectural categories.

## Governance Layers

Responsible for

- ingestion
- normalization
- identity
- quality
- governance

These layers never generate intelligence.

---

## Observation Layer

Responsible for identifying business narratives discussed within one filing.

Owner:

Themes

---

## Business Understanding Layer

Responsible for describing how the business works according to one filing.

Owner:

Structured Intelligence

---

## Business Interpretation Layer

Responsible for explaining what happened this period in business context.

Owner:

Quarter Understanding

---

## Ownership Layer

Responsible for answering investor ownership questions.

Owner:

Investor Intelligence

---

## Presentation Layer

Responsible only for presenting upstream intelligence.

Owner:

Partner Domain

Partner Domain never generates intelligence.

---

# 7. Execution Models

The platform intentionally separates deterministic processing from semantic reasoning.

| Execution Model | Layers |
|-----------------|--------|
| Deterministic | Extraction, Normalization, Filing Artifact, Evidence Identity, Themes Quality, Topic Assignment, Topic Evolution, Company Knowledge Candidate, Governance Promotion, Company Knowledge, Quarter Change, Business Signals, Trust Pillars, Trust Signals |
| LLM | Themes, Structured Intelligence, Quarter Understanding, Investor Intelligence |

LLM layers never perform governance.

Deterministic layers never perform semantic reasoning.

---

# 8. Golden Rule

Every layer answers exactly one question.

No layer answers a downstream question.

If a layer can be removed without losing architectural responsibility, the architecture is incorrect.

If two layers answer the same question, the architecture is incorrect.

Layer ownership is exclusive.

Responsibility never overlaps.
